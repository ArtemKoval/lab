# Nimbus OpsDesk: Operations Wording

Version 2026.1, for the sites on an SLA bought or renewed on or after October 1, 2026.

This document explains what the OpsDesk does for a site, what it escalates and how it estimates the work. The site's SLA record shows its tier and the dates of its term. The desk works in engineer-hours, and no money moves anywhere in this wording.

## 1. Your SLA tier

| | Standard | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Response target | 8 working hours | 2 working hours |
| Weekly capacity | 16 hours | 40 hours |
| On-call margin | none | times 1.5 |

Working hours are 07:00 to 19:00 in the site's time zone, Monday to Friday, without public holidays. A response target counts in hours from the site's report to the first reply from the desk. The weekly capacity is the most effort the desk estimates for one escalation on that tier.

## 2. Escalations, not money

The desk moves work, and it never pays money. The desk escalates a fault the site cannot resolve to the platform team, with an estimate of the effort in engineer-hours. A request for money, such as a refund or a bill query, goes to finance, and the desk declines the escalation and says where the request belongs.

## 3. What the desk escalates

The desk escalates a fault that meets all of these:

- The fault stops the site's clinical work, or stops the site opening.
- The site cannot fix the fault, and a restart or a cleaning does not clear it.
- The fault needs a change the platform team owns, such as provisioning, drivers or the wireless controller.

The desk does not escalate a fault the site can work around with the backup equipment the site holds, and it does not escalate a fault the next vendor window will fix for free.

## 4. The effort estimate

The desk estimates the effort of an escalation in engineer-hours, to half an hour. The estimate carries each work item the desk judges, with its hours. A tier that carries an on-call margin adds the margin's share over two hours of work. The estimate then stays within the tier's weekly capacity. The site's report may add up its own hours first; the desk judges each item and may leave one out.

## 5. Filing an escalation

A site files an escalation within 14 days of the incident. The report names what stopped, when it started, what the site tried, and what stopped working with it. The desk answers an escalation outside the window with a decline, and the reason says the window and the filing date it needs.
