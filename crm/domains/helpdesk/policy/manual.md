# FastFix Support Handling Manual

Version 2026.1. Internal. For support agents and leads, and for any system that decides tickets on their behalf.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This manual explains how FastFix decides support tickets. The support wording is the contract with the customer and it always comes first. This manual covers what the wording leaves open: how to read a clause when a ticket does not fit it neatly, which evidence to ask for, how to work out the credit, and when to pass a ticket to a lead.

Everything here applies to tickets under wording version 2026.1. All amounts are in US dollars.

## 2. The three decisions

Every ticket ends in one of three decisions.

**Resolve with a credit.** An outage of our platform or a response target we missed caused the incident, the evidence is on file and the credit has been worked out. A resolution always carries a credit amount, which can be lower than the amount claimed.

**Close.** The wording gives no credit. The usual reasons are a fault in the customer's own equipment or settings, planned maintenance, a service we do not run, or an incident outside the support term. A close always names the section of the wording it relies on.

**Escalate.** The ticket needs a lead's judgment or more information before it can be resolved or closed. Escalate when a piece of evidence is missing, when the story and the records disagree, when the ticket is the customer's third incident in six months, or when the wording and this manual together still do not settle the question. An escalation always says what is needed and who should look at it.

Choose escalate only for those reasons. A ticket that is clearly covered should be resolved and a ticket that is clearly excluded should be closed, even when the amount is large or the story is unusual.

## 3. Credit authority

A credit of up to $200 can be released by whoever decided the ticket, agent or system. A credit above $200 needs a support lead's sign-off before payment. The decision on the ticket stays resolve, with the credit amount, and the payment waits for the sign-off. The lead who signs off reads the decision and the calculation and records their name against the payment. The limit applies to the credit after the multiplier and the cap, and the amount claimed does not matter.

## 4. Working a ticket

Work every ticket in the same order.

1. Read the customer's story and the lines they claim.
2. Look up the support plan. Note the plan, the response target, the per-incident cap and the multiplier.
3. Check that the incident falls inside the support term and that the ticket was filed within 30 days.
4. Read the status page report or the ticket timeline for the incident.
5. Work out the credit (section 10).
6. Check the customer's incident history (section 9).
7. Record the decision, the credit, the section of the wording relied on and a reason in plain language.

## 5. Evidence and evidence requests

An outage claim needs the status page report. A missed response claim needs the ticket timeline. Our records beat the customer's estimate, but a customer's monitoring graph can name the time of an incident on their side.

When a piece of evidence is missing, escalate the ticket with a request that names it. Do not close a ticket because evidence is missing, and do not resolve it on the customer's promise to send it later. An evidence request gives the customer 14 days, and the ticket stays open while it waits.

## 6. Reading the plans

The Standard plan carries an 8 business hour response target, a $150 per-incident cap and no multiplier. The Priority plan carries a 2 hour response target around the clock, a $400 per-incident cap and a 1.5 multiplier. Always read the plan before you decide, because the same incident can be credited on one plan and not on the other.

## 7. Outages and missed responses

A platform outage runs from the start time on the status page to the time it shows as resolved. Credit each hour or part of an hour at $60. A missed response is a first reply more than five minutes late, and it credits a flat $20. The update note on the status page is part of the record, and an update that breaks the customer's software is our fault when the note says so.

## 8. When the story and the records disagree

Sometimes the records do not support the story. Times do not line up, a graph covers the wrong day, or the ticket timeline shows a reply the customer says never came. Never close a ticket for suspected dishonesty and never accuse a customer of it. Escalate the ticket to the support lead, list each point where the story and the records differ, and say nothing to the customer beyond the fact of a review. Small differences are normal and are not a reason to escalate.

## 9. Repeat incidents

Before any decision, check the customer's incident history. A customer's third incident within any six month period goes to a support lead before payment, whatever its merits. Count incidents by the date the ticket was filed, and count every ticket, credited, closed or open. Most third incidents are resolved after the lead's review, and the referral should say whether the ticket appears payable and give the amount. A first or second incident in six months is decided in the normal way.

## 10. How we work out your credit

Follow the order in section 10 of the wording.

1. Add the amount of each line on the ticket.
2. Take off any credit already issued for the same incident.
3. On the Priority plan, multiply the result by 1.5.
4. Cap the result at the plan's per-incident cap.
5. If nothing is left, close the ticket and explain why in the letter.

**Worked example 1.** A Standard plan customer claims a two hour outage at $60 an hour and one missed response at $20. The lines add to $140. No credit was issued before, the plan carries no multiplier, and the total stays inside the $150 cap. The credit is $140 and no sign-off is needed.

**Worked example 2.** A Priority plan customer claims a five hour outage at $60 an hour and a missed response at $20. The lines add to $320, the multiplier takes it to $480 and the $400 cap takes off $80. The credit is $400, which is above $200, so payment needs a lead's sign-off.

**Worked example 3.** A customer claims $200 and we issued a $250 goodwill credit by phone for the same incident. The credit already issued comes off, the result is below zero and nothing is left to credit. The ticket is closed and the letter explains the calculation.

## 11. Customer data

A customer record holds a name, contact details and an incident history. A ticket often holds facts about the customer's business as well.

- Use a customer's data only to decide that customer's tickets.
- Do not include one customer's details in anything written to or about another customer.
- Do not copy customer data into notes, tools or systems outside the support platform.

## 12. Writing to the customer

Start with the decision and the amount. Show the calculation whenever the credit is lower than the amount claimed, line by line. When closing, name the section of the wording and quote the sentence that applies. When escalating, say exactly what is needed and who will look at it. Be plain, be brief and be kind.
